About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Geology at the Department of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences, University of Houston[cite: 1, 2], working with Prof. Shuhab D. Khan[cite: 2, 4]. My doctoral research is focused on multi-scale detection and exploration of critical minerals using advanced remote sensing and hyperspectral imaging techniques[cite: 2, 4].

My work integrates satellite-based hyperspectral data (PRISMA), ground-based spectroradiometer instruments (ASD FieldSpec Pro), geochemical instrumentation (pXRF, LIBS, XRD), and machine learning to map critical minerals (such as Lithium-Cesium-Tantalum pegmatites and Rare Earth Elements in coal/ash)[cite: 2, 4].

Prior to my PhD, I worked as a Geologist on mineral exploration (copper targeting in North Waziristan), geotechnical mapping, and geohazard evaluations across complex geological terrains[cite: 1].

Research Highlights

Hyperspectral Exploration for LCT Pegmatites
Shigar Valley, Karakoram Range[cite: 4]

Integrating PRISMA hyperspectral spaceborne sensors with VNIR-SWIR-LWIR field spectroscopy and laboratory geochemistry to detect lithium-bearing pegmatites in rugged alpine terrains[cite: 2, 4].

Rare Earth Elements (REE) in Unconventional Sources
Navajo Mine Coal & Fly Ash[cite: 2, 3]

Developing non-destructive spectral characterization workflows to detect and quantify REE distributions in coal and ash byproducts[cite: 2, 4].
